Why Is 25% the Basic Take Point When a Double Is Offered?

Beginner
Intermediate

Learn why a backgammon player can correctly take a double with only a 25% chance to win in a simplified money game, how taking compares with passing, and why gammons, cube ownership, future redoubles, and match score can move the real take point away from 25%.

The short answer

  • Passing loses the current one-point stake.
  • Taking means continuing the game at a cube value of 2.
  • Under the simplified assumptions, taking and passing have equal value at the 25% boundary.
  • Taking is correct above the boundary and passing is correct below it.
  • Taking is not the same as predicting a win.
  • VERIFY: decide how the exact boundary should be described after notation is accepted.

Key idea

A take is not a prediction that you will win. It is a comparison between continuing at the doubled stake and surrendering the current stake.

Why 25% appears

Passing

  • Passing ends the game immediately.
  • The player loses the current one-point stake.
  • This is the result that taking must be compared with.

Taking and winning

  • The game continues with the cube at 2.
  • A win gains two points in the simplified comparison.
  • The larger reward is why taking can be worthwhile even when winning is less likely.

Taking and losing

  • The game continues with the cube at 2.
  • A loss costs two points in the simplified comparison.
  • The player accepts this larger risk instead of surrendering one point immediately.

The decision boundary

  • At the boundary, taking and passing have the same value.
  • Above the boundary, continuing is worth more than passing.
  • Below the boundary, passing is worth more than continuing.

Decision boundary

The point where the two available choices have equal value. The technical term take point becomes useful after this comparison is understood.

What the simplified model leaves out

  • Gammons and backgammons.
  • Ownership of the cube after taking.
  • Future redoubles.
  • Match score.
  • Position-specific volatility.
  • Positions with the same winning chance but different outcome distributions.

Each omitted feature matters because it can change what winning, losing, or owning the next cube decision is worth.

What to remember

  • Twenty-five percent is the basic take point only under simplified assumptions.
  • Taking compares playing on with passing, not winning with losing.
  • A correct take can still be expected to lose most of the time.
  • Gammons, future redoubles, cube ownership, and match score can move the real take point.
